## Start with the idea

Imagine a mixture containing three different compounds. You want to separate them so that you can study each one. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) does this by moving the mixture through a column with a liquid mobile phase.

The compounds do not interact with the column in exactly the same way. Some move through faster; others spend more time interacting with the stationary phase. This difference helps separate them.

HPLC is used in pharmaceutical analysis to separate, identify and quantify components in a sample under a defined method.

## 1. The two phases

| Part | Meaning | Easy way to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile phase | Liquid that carries the sample through the system | The moving carrier |
| Stationary phase | Material held inside the column | The surface that interacts with compounds |

A compound's retention depends on its interaction with both phases and on the method conditions. Do not assume that every compound with a stronger stationary-phase interaction always behaves identically across different columns and mobile phases.

### Main components

- **Solvent reservoir:** stores the mobile phase.
- **Pump:** moves the mobile phase through the system at a controlled flow rate.
- **Injector / autosampler:** introduces a measured sample.
- **Column:** contains the stationary phase and performs the separation.
- **Detector:** converts the presence of eluting compounds into a measurable signal.
- **Data system:** records and displays the detector response.

## 3. How to read a chromatogram

A chromatogram is a plot of detector response against time.

- The horizontal axis usually represents time.
- The vertical axis represents detector response.
- Each peak may represent a component detected as it leaves the column.
- Retention time is the time from injection to the peak's elution point, commonly measured at the peak maximum.
- Peak area is commonly used for quantitative analysis when the method has been appropriately calibrated and validated.

A peak is not automatically proof of a compound's identity. Identification depends on the analytical method, reference standards, system suitability and other supporting evidence.

## 4. Important terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Retention time (tR) | Time taken for a component to elute from the column |
| Peak area | Area under a chromatographic peak; commonly used for quantitation |
| Peak height | Maximum detector response of a peak |
| Resolution | How well two neighbouring peaks are separated |
| Mobile phase | Liquid moving through the column |
| Stationary phase | Material held in the column |
| Isocratic elution | Mobile-phase composition remains constant during the run |
| Gradient elution | Mobile-phase composition changes according to a programmed profile |

## 5. Why does HPLC matter in pharmacy?

HPLC methods can be used for:

- Assay of active pharmaceutical ingredients.
- Related-substances and impurity profiling.
- Stability-indicating analysis.
- Analysis of dissolution samples.
- Content uniformity and formulation studies.
- Testing of raw materials and finished products.

The exact method depends on the drug, formulation, purpose of the test and applicable pharmacopoeial or validated procedure.

## 6. A simple example

Suppose a sample contains compounds A, B and C. Under one chosen method, A leaves the column first, B next and C last.

The chromatogram may show three separate peaks at different retention times. If the peaks are sufficiently separated and the method is suitable, the analyst can use reference standards and calibration to identify and quantify the compounds.

If two peaks overlap, the method may not separate those components adequately. The method may need optimisation, for example by changing the mobile-phase composition, column chemistry, flow or temperature, as appropriate.

## 7. GPAT quick revision

- HPLC uses a **liquid mobile phase**.
- The **column** performs the separation.
- The **pump** moves mobile phase through the system.
- The **detector** senses compounds as they elute.
- A **chromatogram** plots detector response against time.
- Retention time supports identification only under controlled method conditions.
- Peak area is commonly used for quantitation with an appropriate analytical procedure.

<Callout type="exam" title="Common confusion">
The pump does not separate the sample; it delivers the mobile phase. The column is where separation occurs. The detector senses the compounds after they leave the column.
</Callout>
