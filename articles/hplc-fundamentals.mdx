# HPLC Fundamentals

High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) is an instrumental separation technique used to separate, identify and quantify components in a mixture.

## Principle

HPLC separates compounds because they distribute differently between two phases:

- **Mobile phase:** a liquid that carries the sample through the column.
- **Stationary phase:** material packed inside the column that interacts with sample components.

Components with stronger interactions with the stationary phase generally move more slowly and have longer retention times.

## Main components

1. **Solvent reservoir** — stores the mobile phase.
2. **Pump** — delivers mobile phase at controlled high pressure and flow.
3. **Injector** — introduces a measured sample volume.
4. **Column** — contains the stationary phase and performs separation.
5. **Detector** — senses compounds as they leave the column.
6. **Data system** — displays the chromatogram and supports analysis.

## Important chromatographic terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Retention time (tR) | Time taken by a component to reach the detector |
| Peak area | Commonly used for quantitative estimation |
| Resolution | Degree of separation between two peaks |
| Mobile phase | Liquid flowing through the column |
| Stationary phase | Phase held in the column |

## Pharmaceutical applications

- Assay of active pharmaceutical ingredients
- Related-substances and impurity profiling
- Stability-indicating methods
- Dissolution sample analysis
- Content uniformity and formulation studies

## GPAT revision points

- HPLC uses a **liquid mobile phase**.
- The pump is required to move solvent through a packed column.
- Retention time supports identification when method conditions are controlled.
- Peak area is commonly related to the amount of analyte.

## Check your understanding

**Question:** Which HPLC component is primarily responsible for separation?

**Answer:** The column containing the stationary phase.
