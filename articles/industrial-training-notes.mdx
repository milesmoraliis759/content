## Start with the idea

Classroom learning explains what a machine or process is designed to do. Industrial training helps a student see how that idea fits into a controlled manufacturing environment.

One important lesson is that pharmaceutical production is not simply a sequence of machines. Each stage has a purpose, defined instructions, checks and records.

## 1. A typical wet-granulation flow

**Dispensing → Sifting → Dry mixing → Binder addition / granulation → Wet screening (if specified) → Drying → Sizing → Blending → Lubrication → Compression**

The exact sequence and equipment depend on the product, validated process and approved batch manufacturing record. Some processes use different equipment or include additional steps.

## 2. Equipment I wanted to understand

### Rapid Mixer Granulator (RMG)

The RMG is commonly used for dry mixing and wet massing. The impeller promotes mixing and binder distribution; a chopper may help break oversized wet masses. Process parameters such as impeller speed, chopper operation, binder addition rate and endpoint are product-specific.

### Multi Mill

A Multi Mill is a size-reduction machine. Depending on the equipment configuration and approved process, it may be used for wet or dry milling. Screen selection and operating conditions influence particle-size distribution.

### Sifter

A sifter separates material according to specified screen openings. Correct screen identity, integrity, cleanliness and status are essential before use.

### Fluid Bed Dryer (FBD)

An FBD dries granules by passing controlled heated air through the product bed. Inlet temperature, airflow, product temperature and drying endpoint must follow approved process instructions.

### Blender

A blender supports mixing of granules with other formulation ingredients. Blend uniformity depends on loading, mixing time, equipment design, material properties and validated operating conditions.

## 3. The GMP mindset

- Follow the approved SOP and batch manufacturing record.
- Verify equipment status and cleanliness before use.
- Check material identity and status before dispensing or charging.
- Record activities at the time they are performed.
- Report deviations rather than silently correcting records.
- Never assume that two products use identical process settings.

## 4. Questions worth asking during training

- What is the purpose of this processing step?
- Which quality attribute is affected by this parameter?
- How is the endpoint determined and documented?
- What happens when a deviation occurs?
- How is equipment cleaning status verified?

## Reflection

Training is most useful when you ask why a parameter is controlled, what could go wrong if it changes, and where the evidence is recorded.
