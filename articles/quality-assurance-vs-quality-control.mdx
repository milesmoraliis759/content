# Quality Assurance vs Quality Control

Quality is not created only at the end of manufacturing. It is built into the process and verified through appropriate controls.

## Quality Assurance (QA)

**Quality Assurance** is the broader system of planned and systematic activities intended to provide confidence that a product will meet quality requirements.

Typical QA responsibilities may include:
- Quality management system oversight
- SOP and controlled-document management
- Deviation, change-control and CAPA systems
- Supplier qualification and audit programmes
- Batch-record review and disposition processes
- Training and quality-system governance

The exact responsibilities and reporting structure vary by organisation.

## Quality Control (QC)

**Quality Control** focuses on sampling, specifications, testing and the associated decisions about whether materials or products meet defined quality requirements.

Typical QC activities include:
- Raw-material and packaging-material testing
- In-process and finished-product testing
- Analytical method execution
- Stability testing
- Laboratory documentation and data review
- Environmental or microbiological testing where applicable

## QA vs QC comparison

| Aspect | QA | QC |
|---|---|---|
| Broad focus | Quality system and prevention | Testing and verification |
| Typical scope | Organisation-wide processes | Samples, tests and specifications |
| Examples | SOP governance, deviations, CAPA | Assay, dissolution, identification |
| Main question | Is the system designed and followed to achieve quality? | Does this sample meet its specification? |

A common memory aid is: **QA builds confidence in the system; QC tests whether defined requirements are met.** This is simplified; both functions include preventive and detective controls.

## In-process quality assurance (IPQA)

IPQA refers to quality oversight and checks performed during manufacturing and packaging operations. Depending on approved procedures, this may include line clearance, status checks, review of critical process steps and verification that records are completed correctly.

Production personnel remain responsible for performing work according to approved instructions. QA oversight does not replace operator responsibility.

## Documentation and data integrity

Good documentation practices support traceability. Entries should be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, with other applicable ALCOA+ principles such as complete, consistent, enduring and available.

Never backdate, falsify or silently overwrite a record. Follow the site's approved correction and deviation procedures.

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which activity is most directly associated with QC?**

A. SOP governance  
B. Finished-product assay testing  
C. Supplier audit programme  
D. Change-control governance

**Answer: B.** Finished-product assay is a laboratory test against a defined specification.

**Q2. Which statement best describes QA?**

A. QA only performs chemical analysis  
B. QA is limited to final product inspection  
C. QA provides a planned system to support consistent quality  
D. QA replaces production operators

**Answer: C.** QA concerns the broader system of planned and systematic quality activities.
