## Start with the idea

Think about a medicine factory. One team helps build and maintain the quality system. Another team performs defined tests on materials and products. They work together, but they do not do exactly the same job.

That is the simplest way to begin understanding **Quality Assurance (QA)** and **Quality Control (QC)**.

Quality is not created only at the end of manufacturing. It is built into the process and checked through appropriate controls.

## 1. Quality Assurance (QA)

Quality Assurance is the broader system of planned and systematic activities intended to provide confidence that a product will meet quality requirements.

Typical QA responsibilities may include:

- Quality management system oversight.
- SOP and controlled-document management.
- Deviation, change-control and CAPA systems.
- Supplier qualification and audit programmes.
- Batch-record review and disposition processes.
- Training and quality-system governance.

The exact responsibilities and reporting structure vary by organisation.

## 2. Quality Control (QC)

Quality Control focuses on sampling, specifications, testing and the associated decisions about whether materials or products meet defined quality requirements.

Typical QC activities include:

- Raw-material and packaging-material testing.
- In-process and finished-product testing.
- Analytical method execution.
- Stability testing.
- Laboratory documentation and data review.
- Environmental or microbiological testing where applicable.

## 3. QA vs QC: compare the main idea

| Aspect | QA | QC |
|---|---|---|
| Broad focus | Quality system and prevention | Testing and verification |
| Typical scope | Organisation-wide processes | Samples, tests and specifications |
| Examples | SOP governance, deviations, CAPA | Assay, dissolution, identification |
| Main question | Is the system designed and followed to achieve quality? | Does this sample meet its specification? |

A useful memory aid is: **QA builds confidence in the system; QC tests whether defined requirements are met.**

This is a simplified distinction. Both functions can include preventive and detective controls.

## 4. What is IPQA?

In-process Quality Assurance (IPQA) refers to quality oversight and checks performed during manufacturing and packaging operations.

Depending on approved procedures, this may include:

- Line clearance.
- Equipment and area status checks.
- Verification of critical process steps.
- Review of in-process records.
- Checks that approved instructions are being followed.

Production personnel remain responsible for performing their work according to approved instructions. QA oversight does not replace operator responsibility.

## 5. Documentation and data integrity

Good documentation supports traceability. Entries should be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate. Other ALCOA+ principles commonly include complete, consistent, enduring and available.

Never backdate, falsify or silently overwrite a record. Follow the site's approved correction and deviation procedures.

## 6. A practical example

Imagine a tablet batch is being compressed.

- **Production** operates the compression machine according to the approved batch record.
- **IPQA** may perform the checks and oversight defined in the site's procedure.
- **QC** tests samples according to the approved specification and method.
- **QA** reviews the relevant quality records and oversees the quality-system process.

This is an educational example. The actual division of work is defined by the site's procedures and applicable requirements.

<Callout type="note" title="Remember">
A successful laboratory result does not replace control of the manufacturing process. A good quality system needs both appropriate process controls and appropriate testing.
</Callout>
