# GMP and pharmaceutical quality systems

**Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)** are requirements and principles intended to ensure that medicines are consistently produced and controlled to appropriate quality standards.

The central idea: quality must be built into the process, not tested only at the end.

## 1. Core GMP principles

- Suitable premises, equipment and utilities.
- Trained personnel and defined responsibilities.
- Approved procedures and controlled manufacturing instructions.
- Qualified equipment and validated processes where required.
- Prevention of contamination and mix-ups.
- Complete, accurate and traceable records.
- Investigation of deviations and implementation of effective CAPA.
- Independent quality oversight.

## 2. Documentation hierarchy

| Document / record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| SOP | Describes an approved routine procedure |
| Specification | Defines tests and acceptance criteria for materials or products |
| Master formula / manufacturing instruction | Defines the approved manufacturing formula and process |
| Batch manufacturing record | Records what actually happened for a specific batch |
| Batch packaging record | Records packaging operations and reconciliation |
| Logbook | Records equipment / area use, cleaning, maintenance or other defined activities |
| Protocol | Defines planned qualification, validation or study activities |
| Report | Summarises execution, results, deviations and conclusions |

<Callout type="exam" title="SOP vs record">
An SOP tells personnel **how an activity should be performed**. A completed record provides evidence of **what was actually performed**.
</Callout>

## 3. Data integrity

A widely taught data-integrity framework is ALCOA+:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Attributable | Who performed or recorded the activity is identifiable |
| Legible | Records can be read and understood |
| Contemporaneous | Recorded at the time the activity occurs |
| Original | Original record or a verified true copy |
| Accurate | Correct and free from unjustified alteration |
| Complete | All relevant data are retained |
| Consistent | Events follow a traceable sequence |
| Enduring | Records remain available for the required retention period |
| Available | Records can be retrieved when needed |

## 4. Deviation, change control and CAPA

- **Deviation:** departure from an approved procedure, instruction or expected condition.
- **Change control:** formal assessment and approval of a proposed change before implementation, with appropriate review after implementation.
- **CAPA:** corrective and preventive action. Corrective action addresses an identified problem or its cause; preventive action aims to prevent potential recurrence or occurrence.

<ProcessFlow title="A simplified deviation and CAPA cycle">
<ol>
<li>Identify, document and contain the deviation.</li>
<li>Assess immediate product, process and patient-quality impact.</li>
<li>Investigate using evidence and appropriate root-cause tools.</li>
<li>Define corrective and preventive actions with owners and due dates.</li>
<li>Implement and document approved actions.</li>
<li>Verify effectiveness and close through the quality system.</li>
</ol>
</ProcessFlow>

## 5. Qualification vs validation

| Term | Main question |
|---|---|
| Design qualification (DQ) | Is the proposed design suitable for its intended purpose? |
| Installation qualification (IQ) | Is the equipment / system installed as specified? |
| Operational qualification (OQ) | Does it operate as intended across defined operating ranges? |
| Performance qualification (PQ) | Does it perform effectively under routine or representative conditions? |
| Process validation | Does the manufacturing process consistently deliver product meeting predetermined quality requirements? |

<Callout type="note" title="Qualification and validation are related">
Equipment qualification provides documented evidence that equipment is suitable. Process validation provides documented evidence that the process can consistently achieve its intended result. The precise lifecycle approach depends on applicable regulations and guidance.
</Callout>

## GPAT / industry quick revision

- GMP = consistent manufacture and control of quality medicines.
- SOP = approved instruction; batch record = evidence of execution.
- ALCOA+ = data integrity principles.
- Deviation = departure from approved expectation.
- CAPA = corrective and preventive action.
- IQ = installed correctly; OQ = operates correctly; PQ = performs as required.
- Validation is evidence-based and documented.

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which document records the actual operations performed for a specific batch?**

A. SOP  
B. Batch manufacturing record  
C. Training calendar  
D. Product brochure

**Answer: B.** The batch manufacturing record documents the execution and relevant results for a particular batch.

**Q2. In ALCOA, the letter C stands for:**

A. Confidential  
B. Contemporaneous  
C. Calibrated  
D. Certified

**Answer: B.** Data should be recorded at the time the activity occurs.

**Q3. Which qualification stage checks that equipment is installed according to specification?**

A. IQ  
B. OQ  
C. PQ  
D. Process validation

**Answer: A.** Installation qualification documents that installation meets approved requirements.
