# Antitubercular Drugs: An Overview

Tuberculosis (TB) treatment uses combinations of medicines to improve effectiveness and reduce the risk of resistance. The exact regimen depends on disease site, drug susceptibility, patient characteristics and current national or international guidance.

## Common first-line agents

The classic first-line drug set commonly taught in pharmacy curricula includes isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol.

| Drug | Simplified mechanism | Important safety association |
|---|---|---|
| Isoniazid (INH) | Prodrug activated by mycobacterial KatG; inhibits mycolic-acid synthesis through InhA pathway | Hepatotoxicity; peripheral neuropathy risk |
| Rifampicin | Inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase | Hepatotoxicity; enzyme induction; orange-red body-fluid discoloration |
| Pyrazinamide | Prodrug converted by bacterial enzymes to active pyrazinoic acid; activity depends on conditions in the bacillus and its environment | Hepatotoxicity; hyperuricaemia |
| Ethambutol | Inhibits arabinosyl transferases involved in cell-wall arabinogalactan synthesis | Optic neuritis; visual acuity and colour-vision monitoring |

## Why combinations are used

Mycobacterium tuberculosis populations may contain organisms with different metabolic states and spontaneous resistant mutants. Combining medicines with different targets helps improve sterilising activity and reduce selection of resistance.

The regimen is not a simple list to memorise: duration, dose, susceptibility results and monitoring all matter.

## High-yield comparisons

- **INH:** mycolic acid synthesis.
- **Rifampicin:** RNA polymerase.
- **Pyrazinamide:** prodrug; pyrazinoic acid; acidic intracellular environments are classically discussed.
- **Ethambutol:** arabinosyl transferase and cell-wall synthesis.

## Safety and monitoring concepts

- Isoniazid-associated neuropathy risk is related to pyridoxine metabolism; pyridoxine supplementation is recommended for selected risk groups under clinical guidance.
- Rifampicin is a potent enzyme inducer and can interact with many medicines.
- Liver injury can occur with several antitubercular drugs.
- Ethambutol-associated visual symptoms require prompt clinical assessment.

Never stop or alter TB treatment on the basis of a study note. Regimens and monitoring should follow current national TB programme guidance and a qualified clinician.

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which antitubercular drug inhibits mycolic-acid synthesis?**

A. Ethambutol  
B. Isoniazid  
C. Rifampicin  
D. Streptomycin

**Answer: B.** Isoniazid is activated by KatG and inhibits the mycolic-acid synthesis pathway.

**Q2. Which drug is associated with inhibition of DNA-dependent RNA polymerase?**

A. Rifampicin  
B. Pyrazinamide  
C. Ethambutol  
D. Isoniazid

**Answer: A.** Rifampicin binds the bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
