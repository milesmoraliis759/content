# Sulfonamide antibacterial agents

Sulfonamides are synthetic antibacterial agents that interfere with bacterial folate synthesis. They are structural analogues of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA), a substrate used by bacteria to synthesise folate.

## 1. Mechanism of action

<ProcessFlow title="Folate pathway and sulfonamide action">
<ol>
<li>Bacteria use PABA as a substrate in folate synthesis.</li>
<li>Dihydropteroate synthase (DHPS) incorporates PABA into the pathway.</li>
<li>Sulfonamides compete with PABA at DHPS.</li>
<li>Formation of dihydropteroic acid and downstream folate is reduced.</li>
<li>Reduced tetrahydrofolate availability limits nucleotide synthesis and bacterial growth.</li>
</ol>
</ProcessFlow>

<Callout type="exam" title="Target enzyme">
Sulfonamides inhibit **dihydropteroate synthase (DHPS)**. Trimethoprim acts at a different step by inhibiting bacterial dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR).
</Callout>

## 2. Core structural features

The classical antibacterial sulfonamide pharmacophore contains:
- A para-amino group on the benzene ring (N⁴ amino group).
- A sulfonamide group (–SO₂NH–) at the para position relative to the amino group.
- A heterocyclic substituent on the sulfonamide nitrogen (N¹) in many clinically used agents.

## 3. SAR: what changes activity?

| Structural feature | General significance |
|---|---|
| Para-amino (N⁴) group | Important for resemblance to PABA and antibacterial activity |
| Para relationship | The classical arrangement is important for activity |
| Sulfonamide group | Essential pharmacophoric region |
| Substitution at N¹ | Heterocyclic groups can alter acidity, ionisation and pharmacokinetic properties |
| Modification of N⁴ | Often reduces activity unless the group can be converted back to the free amino form in vivo |

<Callout type="caution" title="Do not overgeneralise">
SAR rules describe the classical antibacterial sulfonamide series. Prodrugs and specialised sulfonamides may behave differently; do not apply the antibacterial pharmacophore to every medicine containing a sulfonamide group.
</Callout>

## 4. Examples and combinations

| Drug | Key point |
|---|---|
| Sulfamethoxazole | Commonly combined with trimethoprim |
| Sulfadiazine | A systemic antibacterial sulfonamide |
| Sulfacetamide | Used topically in selected ophthalmic preparations |
| Sulfadoxine | Long-acting sulfonamide; used in specific combination regimens |

**Co-trimoxazole** combines sulfamethoxazole and trimethoprim. The combination produces sequential blockade of folate metabolism: DHPS inhibition followed by DHFR inhibition.

## 5. Resistance and adverse effects

- Resistance may arise through altered DHPS, increased PABA production or reduced drug accumulation.
- Important adverse effects include hypersensitivity reactions, photosensitivity and crystalluria; severe cutaneous reactions and blood dyscrasias are uncommon but serious.
- Risk depends on the individual drug, dose, patient factors and clinical context.

## GPAT quick revision

- Sulfonamides are PABA analogues.
- Target: dihydropteroate synthase.
- They are generally bacteriostatic when used alone.
- Trimethoprim inhibits dihydrofolate reductase.
- Co-trimoxazole = sulfamethoxazole + trimethoprim.
- N⁴ para-amino group is a key feature of the classical pharmacophore.
