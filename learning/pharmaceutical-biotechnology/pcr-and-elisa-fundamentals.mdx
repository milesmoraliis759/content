## Start with the idea

PCR and ELISA are two different ways to look for biological material. PCR makes many copies of a chosen DNA region, while ELISA uses antibody binding to detect a target.

PCR and ELISA are widely used laboratory techniques, but they answer different questions.

- **PCR** amplifies a selected nucleic-acid sequence.
- **ELISA** detects an analyte, commonly an antigen or antibody, using specific binding and an enzyme-linked signal.

## 1. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

<Definition term="PCR">Polymerase chain reaction is an in-vitro method for amplifying a specific DNA sequence using primers, a thermostable DNA polymerase, nucleotides and repeated temperature cycles.</Definition>

### Three core steps

<ProcessFlow title="One PCR cycle">
<ol>
<li>Denaturation: heating separates double-stranded DNA into single strands.</li>
<li>Annealing: cooling allows primers to bind complementary target sequences.</li>
<li>Extension: thermostable DNA polymerase extends primers and synthesises new DNA strands.</li>
</ol>
</ProcessFlow>

Repeated cycles produce exponential amplification in the idealised model. Actual amplification efficiency is affected by template quality, primer design, enzyme performance and reaction conditions.

### Essential components

- Template DNA.
- Forward and reverse primers.
- Thermostable DNA polymerase, commonly Taq polymerase in conventional PCR.
- dNTPs.
- Buffer and Mg²⁺.
- Thermal cycler.

## 2. ELISA

<Definition term="ELISA">Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay is an immunoassay that uses antigen–antibody binding and an enzyme-mediated signal to detect or quantify an analyte.</Definition>

### Common formats

| Type | Basic arrangement | Typical interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Direct ELISA | Enzyme-labelled primary antibody binds antigen | Signal relates to antigen |
| Indirect ELISA | Primary antibody is detected by enzyme-labelled secondary antibody | Commonly used to detect antibodies |
| Sandwich ELISA | Capture antibody + antigen + detection antibody | Often offers high specificity |
| Competitive ELISA | Sample analyte competes with labelled analyte or binding reagent | Signal is commonly inversely related to analyte amount |

## 3. PCR vs ELISA

| Feature | PCR | ELISA |
|---|---|---|
| Target | DNA / RNA (after reverse transcription for RNA) | Antigen or antibody |
| Main principle | Nucleic-acid amplification | Immunological binding |
| Signal / readout | Amplified nucleic acid, often detected by fluorescence or electrophoresis | Enzyme-generated colour / signal |
| Key reagents | Primers, polymerase, nucleotides | Antibodies, enzyme conjugate, substrate |
| Common use | Genetic testing, pathogen nucleic-acid detection | Serology, antigen detection, protein quantification |

<Callout type="exam" title="RT-PCR distinction">
Reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR) first converts RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA), then amplifies the cDNA. Real-time PCR monitors amplification during cycles; RT-PCR and real-time PCR describe different features and should not be treated as synonyms.
</Callout>

## GPAT quick revision

- PCR: denaturation → annealing → extension.
- Taq polymerase is thermostable.
- Primers define the target region.
- ELISA uses antigen–antibody specificity and an enzyme-linked signal.
- Sandwich ELISA uses capture and detection antibodies.
- Competitive ELISA often gives an inverse signal relationship.
