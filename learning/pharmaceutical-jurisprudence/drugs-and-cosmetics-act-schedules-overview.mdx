# Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules: an overview

The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 form a major part of India's framework for regulating the import, manufacture, distribution and sale of drugs and cosmetics.

The law aims to support the quality, safety and proper control of regulated products. The Rules contain detailed requirements, including licences, labelling, standards and schedules.

<Callout type="caution" title="Check current amendments">
This is a learning summary, not a legal opinion. Schedules and requirements can be amended. For compliance decisions, consult the current official text, amendments, notifications and applicable state / central authority requirements.
</Callout>

## 1. Regulatory structure

- The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) operates under the Directorate General of Health Services, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- The Drugs Controller General (India) (DCGI) leads key central regulatory functions.
- State drug control authorities perform important licensing, inspection and enforcement functions within their jurisdiction.
- The exact allocation of responsibilities depends on the product and activity.

## 2. Selected schedules commonly taught

| Schedule | Broad subject / purpose commonly taught |
|---|---|
| Schedule C and C(1) | Biological and special products, including specified categories |
| Schedule G | Certain drugs to be used under medical supervision; caution labelling requirements |
| Schedule H | Prescription drugs subject to specified sale restrictions |
| Schedule H1 | Specified prescription drugs with additional record-keeping requirements |
| Schedule J | Diseases / ailments for which cure claims are restricted under the Rules |
| Schedule K | Exemptions from certain provisions, subject to conditions |
| Schedule M | Good Manufacturing Practices and requirements of premises, plant and equipment |
| Schedule N | Minimum equipment for running a pharmacy |
| Schedule P | Life period / shelf-life and storage provisions for specified drugs |
| Schedule T | GMP requirements for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines |
| Schedule X | Certain specified drugs subject to stricter controls and record requirements |

## 3. Common exam comparisons

**Schedule M vs Schedule T**

- Schedule M: GMP requirements for pharmaceutical manufacturing under the relevant Rules.
- Schedule T: GMP requirements for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines.

**Schedule H vs Schedule H1 vs Schedule X**

These schedules identify categories of drugs subject to prescription and/or additional sale and record controls. Do not assume that one label alone captures every legal requirement: check the current rule text, labelling conditions and amendments.

## 4. Why schedules matter

Schedules convert broad legal powers into detailed operational requirements. They help define:
- Which products or activities require specific controls.
- Manufacturing and premises standards.
- Prescription, sale and record-keeping conditions.
- Labelling, storage and shelf-life requirements.
- Exemptions and their conditions.

<Callout type="exam" title="High-yield association">
Schedule M → GMP for pharmaceutical manufacturing. Schedule T → GMP for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines. Schedule N → minimum equipment for a pharmacy.
</Callout>
