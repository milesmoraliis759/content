# Dissolution Testing

Dissolution testing measures the rate and extent to which a drug substance goes into solution from a pharmaceutical dosage form under specified conditions.

## Why is it performed?

- Evaluates in-vitro drug release.
- Supports batch-to-batch quality control.
- Helps compare formulations.
- Supports development and regulatory assessment.

## Common USP apparatus

| Apparatus | Common name | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| I | Basket | Capsules and products prone to floating |
| II | Paddle | Immediate-release tablets and many other forms |
| III | Reciprocating cylinder | Modified-release dosage forms |
| IV | Flow-through cell | Special formulations and poorly soluble drugs |

The exact apparatus and conditions must follow the individual product monograph or validated method.

## Key operating parameters

- Dissolution medium and volume
- Temperature
- Agitation speed
- Sampling time points
- Filter suitability
- Analytical method

## Dissolution versus disintegration

| Disintegration | Dissolution |
|---|---|
| Measures breakdown of a dosage form into particles | Measures drug going into solution |
| Does not directly quantify dissolved drug | Quantifies dissolved drug under defined conditions |
| Usually a physical test | A drug-release performance test |

## Remember

A tablet may disintegrate but the drug still needs to dissolve before it can generally be absorbed. In-vitro dissolution is a useful quality and performance test, but it does not by itself guarantee clinical bioavailability.
