# Surface Tension and Interfacial Phenomena

These concepts explain why liquids form droplets, why powders may resist wetting, and why surfactants are useful in pharmaceutical formulations.

## 1. Surface tension

<Definition term="Surface tension">
The tangential force per unit length, or surface energy per unit area, associated with a liquid surface.
</Definition>

**Surface tension** is the force acting per unit length at a liquid surface, or equivalently the surface energy per unit area. It arises because molecules at the surface experience an unbalanced cohesive attraction.

- SI unit: N/m
- Common pharmaceutical unit: mN/m
- Higher cohesive forces generally give higher surface tension.

A liquid tends to minimise its surface area. This is why a small droplet tends to become approximately spherical when other forces are negligible.

## 2. Interfacial tension

**Interfacial tension** is the tension at the boundary between two immiscible phases, such as oil and water.

Do not confuse the two terms:

| Term | Boundary |
|---|---|
| Surface tension | Liquid–gas interface |
| Interfacial tension | Two-phase interface, commonly liquid–liquid |

A surfactant can adsorb at an interface and reduce the energy required to create or maintain that interface.

## 3. Surfactants

A surfactant is an amphiphilic molecule containing:
- A hydrophilic (water-attracting) part
- A lipophilic or hydrophobic (oil-attracting) part

Surfactants may act as wetting agents, detergents, emulsifying agents, solubilising agents or foaming agents depending on their structure and formulation conditions.

### Broad classes

| Class | Typical head-group charge | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Anionic | Negative | Sodium lauryl sulfate |
| Cationic | Positive | Cetrimide |
| Non-ionic | No formal ionic charge | Polysorbates, sorbitan esters |
| Amphoteric | Charge depends on pH/environment | Lecithin and betaines |

Compatibility and suitability depend on route, concentration, excipients and the product's safety requirements.

## 4. Wetting and contact angle

Wetting describes how well a liquid spreads over a solid surface. Contact angle is one way to describe this behaviour.

- Smaller contact angle: generally better wetting.
- Larger contact angle: generally poorer wetting.

Surfactants may improve wetting of hydrophobic powders by reducing the relevant interfacial tension and changing interfacial interactions.

## 5. Critical micelle concentration (CMC)

At low concentration, surfactant molecules tend to adsorb at interfaces. Once the interface becomes substantially populated, additional surfactant molecules increasingly form aggregates called **micelles** in the bulk solution.

The concentration at which micelle formation becomes appreciable is called the critical micelle concentration (CMC).

Important points:
- CMC is characteristic of a surfactant under specified conditions.
- Temperature, electrolytes, solvent composition and other formulation ingredients can affect it.
- Above the CMC, micelles can increase the apparent solubilisation of some poorly water-soluble substances.

A micelle is not a chemical reaction or a new covalent compound; it is a dynamic molecular assembly.

## 6. HLB value

Hydrophilic–lipophilic balance (HLB) is an empirical scale used to help select non-ionic surfactants for formulation work.

As a broad guide:
- Lower HLB: more lipophilic character; often considered for water-in-oil systems.
- Higher HLB: more hydrophilic character; often considered for oil-in-water systems.

The required HLB of the oil phase, surfactant blend, temperature and intended dosage form must also be considered. HLB alone does not guarantee emulsion stability.

## GPAT / NIPER revision points

1. Surface tension relates to a liquid surface; interfacial tension relates to an interface between phases.
2. Surfactants are amphiphilic.
3. CMC marks the concentration region where micellisation becomes appreciable.
4. Micelles can assist solubilisation of suitable hydrophobic molecules.
5. HLB is a formulation-selection aid, not a universal stability predictor.

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which part of a surfactant interacts preferentially with water?**

A. Hydrophilic group  
B. Hydrophobic tail  
C. Non-polar tail only  
D. None of these

**Answer: A.** The hydrophilic group has affinity for the aqueous phase.

**Q2. What is the CMC?**

A. The melting point of a surfactant  
B. The concentration at which micellisation becomes appreciable  
C. The pH of a micellar solution  
D. The concentration at which all surfactant evaporates

**Answer: B.** Above the CMC, additional surfactant increasingly forms micelles in the bulk solution.
