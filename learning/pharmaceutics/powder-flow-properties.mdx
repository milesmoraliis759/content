# Powder Flow Properties

Powder flow is the ability of a powder to move under specified conditions. It affects die filling, capsule filling, blending, transfer and content uniformity.

Powder flow is influenced by particle size, shape, surface roughness, moisture, electrostatic charge, density and cohesion.

## 1. Angle of repose

The angle of repose is the angle formed between the horizontal plane and the surface of a powder cone formed under a defined method.

<Formula label="Angle of repose">
tan θ = h / r
</Formula>

Where:
- θ = angle of repose
- h = height of the powder cone
- r = radius of the cone base

A lower angle often suggests better flow under comparable test conditions, but method and powder properties matter.

## 2. Bulk density and tapped density

**Bulk density** is mass divided by the untapped bulk volume occupied by the powder.

**Tapped density** is mass divided by the volume after a specified tapping procedure.

<Formula label="Density">
Bulk density = Mass / Untapped volume

Tapped density = Mass / Tapped volume
</Formula>

Tapped density is usually equal to or greater than bulk density because tapping reduces the volume occupied by the powder bed.

## 3. Carr's compressibility index

<Formula label="Carr index">
Carr Index (%) = [(Tapped density − Bulk density) / Tapped density] × 100
</Formula>

It estimates the extent to which a powder bed consolidates under tapping. Lower values often indicate better flow, but the interpretation is method- and material-dependent.

## 4. Hausner ratio

<Formula label="Hausner ratio">
Hausner Ratio = Tapped density / Bulk density
</Formula>

A ratio closer to 1 generally indicates less consolidation and often better flow.

## 5. Worked example

A powder has:
- Bulk density = 0.50 g/mL
- Tapped density = 0.625 g/mL

Carr index:

= [(0.625 − 0.50) / 0.625] × 100

= 0.125 / 0.625 × 100

= **20%**

Hausner ratio:

= 0.625 / 0.50

= **1.25**

The values suggest some consolidation on tapping. Interpret the result using the selected method and relevant reference scale rather than treating a single number as a universal pass/fail criterion.

## 6. Why these properties matter in manufacturing

Poor flow can contribute to:
- Inconsistent die filling and tablet mass variation.
- Poor capsule filling consistency.
- Segregation during transfer.
- Manufacturing interruptions and yield loss.

Possible formulation or process approaches include granulation, particle engineering, glidants and moisture control. Any change must be assessed for compatibility and product performance.

<Callout type="tip" title="Remember the direction">
Tapped density is generally higher than bulk density. Therefore, Hausner ratio is generally 1 or greater, and Carr index is generally zero or greater.
</Callout>

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which density is used in the denominator of Carr's index?**

A. Bulk density  
B. Tapped density  
C. True density  
D. Particle density

**Answer: B.** Carr index uses tapped density in the denominator.

**Q2. Bulk density is 0.4 g/mL and tapped density is 0.5 g/mL. What is the Hausner ratio?**

A. 0.8  
B. 1.0  
C. 1.25  
D. 2.0

**Answer: C.** Hausner ratio = 0.5 / 0.4 = 1.25.

**Q3. Which statement is generally correct?**

A. Tapped density is always lower than bulk density  
B. Tapping generally reduces powder-bed volume  
C. Carr index is calculated from tablet hardness  
D. Angle of repose measures drug potency

**Answer: B.** Tapping consolidates the powder bed under the specified procedure.
