## Start with the idea

Tablet compression is the step where powder or granules are squeezed into a solid tablet. The machine must fill, compress and eject each tablet consistently.

Tablet compression converts a uniform powder blend or granule blend into tablets by applying force between punches inside a die.

The aim is to produce tablets with reproducible mass, strength, appearance and drug-release performance. Compression settings are formulation- and machine-dependent.

## 1. Main parts of a tablet compression machine

- **Hopper:** holds the blend before feeding.
- **Feeder:** delivers blend into the die cavity.
- **Die:** defines the tablet diameter and shape.
- **Upper punch:** enters the die from above.
- **Lower punch:** supports the powder bed and controls fill depth / ejection.
- **Compression rollers:** apply the compression force through the punches.
- **Ejection cam / take-off blade:** helps the lower punch raise the tablet for removal.

A rotary press has multiple stations arranged around a rotating turret, allowing repeated die filling, compression and ejection.

## 2. Compression cycle

<ProcessFlow title="A simplified tablet compression cycle">
<ol>
<li><strong>Filling:</strong> the lower punch moves down and blend enters the die cavity.</li>
<li><strong>Metering:</strong> excess powder is removed and fill depth is set.</li>
<li><strong>Pre-compression (if fitted):</strong> a lower initial force helps remove entrapped air and consolidate the powder bed.</li>
<li><strong>Main compression:</strong> upper and lower punches apply the designed compression force.</li>
<li><strong>Decompression:</strong> pressure is released as the punches leave the compression zone.</li>
<li><strong>Ejection:</strong> the lower punch rises and the tablet is pushed out of the die.</li>
<li><strong>Take-off:</strong> the tablet is guided away from the die table.</li>
</ol>
</ProcessFlow>

## 3. Tablet manufacturing approaches

| Method | Basic idea | When it may be considered |
|---|---|---|
| Direct compression | Blend suitable powders and compress without a granulation step | Ingredients have suitable flow and compactability |
| Wet granulation | Use a granulating liquid / binder to form granules, then dry and compress | Improved flow or compactability is needed and moisture / heat are acceptable |
| Dry granulation | Compact powder without liquid, then mill and compress | Moisture or heat exposure should be avoided |

No method is universally best. API properties, excipient functionality, stability, dose, equipment and quality target determine the route.

## 4. Common tablet defects

| Defect | What you see | Possible contributing factors |
|---|---|---|
| Capping | Top or bottom crown separates from tablet | Entrapped air, elastic recovery, formulation or compression conditions |
| Lamination | Tablet separates into layers | Air entrapment, excessive fines, formulation / compression issues |
| Sticking | Material adheres to punch face | Moisture, inadequate lubrication, sticky formulation |
| Picking | Material is pulled from tablet surface and sticks to punch engraving | Localised sticking, moisture or unsuitable formulation |
| Binding | Tablet sticks to die wall during ejection | Inadequate lubrication, rough tooling or formulation factors |
| Chipping | Edges break away | Low mechanical strength, dry / brittle granules or handling |
| Mottling | Uneven colour distribution | Colour distribution, migration or processing issues |
| Double impression | Duplicate embossing / logo impression | Punch rotation or machine / tooling design factors |

<Callout type="caution" title="Troubleshooting principle">
A visible defect can have more than one cause. Do not change machine settings based on a single symptom alone; investigate material attributes, blend uniformity, moisture, tooling condition and process records.
</Callout>

## 5. In-process controls

Depending on the product and approved process, in-process checks can include:
- Tablet appearance and identification.
- Average mass and individual mass variation.
- Thickness and diameter.
- Breaking force.
- Friability and disintegration at defined intervals where required.
- Compression force, turret speed and ejection behaviour.
- Blend / tablet assay or content uniformity according to the control strategy.

The actual sampling frequency and limits must be defined in the approved batch record and product specification.

## 6. GPAT quick distinctions

- **Capping:** separation of a crown from the tablet.
- **Lamination:** separation into two or more layers.
- **Sticking:** adhesion to the punch face.
- **Picking:** localised removal of material, often around punch lettering.
- **Binding:** sticking to the die wall, often noticed during ejection.
