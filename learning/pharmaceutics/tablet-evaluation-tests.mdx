# Evaluation Tests for Tablets

Tablet evaluation checks whether a batch meets its specified physical and performance requirements. Different tests answer different questions; no single test proves overall quality.

## 1. Physical and performance tests

| Test | What it evaluates | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| General appearance | Colour, shape, surface, defects, markings | Visual inspection |
| Dimensions | Thickness and diameter | Dimensional consistency |
| Uniformity of mass / weight variation | Variation in individual tablet mass | Pharmacopoeial procedure and limits apply |
| Tablet breaking force (hardness) | Force required to break a tablet under defined test conditions | Not a direct substitute for dissolution |
| Friability | Tendency to lose mass by abrasion / tumbling | Commonly evaluated using a friabilator |
| Disintegration | Time for a dosage unit to break down under specified conditions | Does not quantify drug dissolved |
| Dissolution | Rate and extent of drug entering solution | Product-specific method and acceptance criteria |

## 2. Friability test

Friability is a measure of a tablet's tendency to chip, crumble or abrade during handling.

A commonly taught expression is:

<Formula label="Percentage friability">
% Friability = [(Initial mass − Final mass) / Initial mass] × 100
</Formula>

For a typical conventional uncoated tablet teaching example, a value around or below 1% is often discussed. **Do not apply that as a universal acceptance limit:** the applicable pharmacopoeial chapter, product monograph and approved specification control.

**Conceptual steps:**
1. Select and dedust the required sample according to the applicable method.
2. Weigh the sample.
3. Operate the friabilator using the specified drum, speed and number of rotations.
4. Remove, dedust and reweigh the tablets.
5. Calculate percentage mass loss and assess against the specification.

<Callout type="exam" title="Do not mix up the tests">
Friability evaluates resistance to abrasion and tumbling. Hardness / breaking force evaluates the force needed to break a tablet. They are related formulation attributes, but they are not the same test.
</Callout>

## 3. Disintegration test

The disintegration apparatus typically has a basket-rack assembly with tubes and a specified screen arrangement. The assembly moves up and down in a liquid medium maintained at the method-specified temperature.

The endpoint is based on the dosage unit breaking down as defined by the applicable method. The test does not measure the concentration of drug dissolved.

## 4. Dissolution test

Dissolution testing measures drug release into a specified medium over time. Common apparatus include:
- **Apparatus 1:** rotating basket.
- **Apparatus 2:** paddle.

Other apparatus may be required for particular dosage forms or products. The method specifies medium, volume, temperature, rotation speed, sampling times, analytical procedure and acceptance criteria.

<Callout type="caution" title="Method-specific conditions">
Do not assume one universal RPM, medium or time limit for every tablet. Use the current applicable pharmacopoeial chapter, monograph and approved product method.
</Callout>

## 5. High-yield comparison

| Question | Correct test |
|---|---|
| Does the tablet resist abrasion? | Friability |
| How much force breaks the tablet? | Breaking force / hardness |
| Does the tablet break down? | Disintegration |
| How much drug enters solution over time? | Dissolution |
| Are tablet masses consistent? | Uniformity of mass |

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which test primarily evaluates a tablet's resistance to abrasion?**

A. Dissolution  
B. Friability  
C. Disintegration  
D. Assay

**Answer: B.** Friability evaluates mass loss due to abrasion and tumbling.

**Q2. Which apparatus is commonly associated with USP dissolution Apparatus 2?**

A. Basket  
B. Paddle  
C. Disintegration basket-rack assembly  
D. Friabilator drum

**Answer: B.** Apparatus 2 is the paddle apparatus.

**Q3. A tablet passes disintegration. Which statement is correct?**

A. It must pass dissolution  
B. Its assay is automatically correct  
C. Drug dissolution still requires evaluation by the applicable method  
D. Its friability is zero

**Answer: C.** Disintegration and dissolution measure different endpoints.

## Revision snapshot

- Friability = abrasion / mass loss.
- Breaking force = force required to break.
- Disintegration = dosage-form breakup.
- Dissolution = drug enters solution.
- Uniformity of mass = individual mass consistency.
