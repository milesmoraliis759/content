# Tablet Excipients

A tablet is not made of the drug alone. Most formulations contain **excipients**: substances added to help manufacturing, stability, release, appearance or patient acceptability.

<Callout type="note" title="Start with the purpose">
The API provides the intended pharmacological effect. Excipients help turn the API into a reproducible, stable and usable dosage form. An excipient is not automatically pharmacologically inactive in every context; its suitability depends on dose, route and patient population.
</Callout>

## 1. Main classes of tablet excipients

| Class | Main function | Common examples |
|---|---|---|
| Diluent / filler | Adds bulk when the drug dose is small | Lactose, microcrystalline cellulose, mannitol, dibasic calcium phosphate |
| Binder | Helps powder particles adhere and form granules/tablets | Povidone (PVP), starch paste, HPMC |
| Disintegrant | Helps a tablet break apart after administration | Starch, sodium starch glycolate, croscarmellose sodium, crospovidone |
| Lubricant | Reduces friction during ejection from the die | Magnesium stearate, stearic acid |
| Glidant | Improves powder flow | Colloidal silicon dioxide, talc |
| Antiadherent | Reduces sticking to punches and dies | Talc, magnesium stearate |
| Colour / opacifier | Improves identification or appearance | Approved colours, titanium dioxide where permitted |
| Sweetener / flavour | Improves palatability, especially in chewable or dispersible products | Sucralose, aspartame, suitable flavours |

## 2. The three roles that are often confused

<Definition term="Binder">
A binder increases cohesion between particles, helping granules or tablets hold together.
</Definition>

<Definition term="Disintegrant">
A disintegrant promotes breakup of the tablet into smaller particles after contact with a suitable fluid.
</Definition>

<Definition term="Lubricant">
A lubricant reduces friction at the tablet–die wall interface and helps tablet ejection.
</Definition>

**Memory line:** Binder holds it together; disintegrant helps it break apart; lubricant helps it come out of the die.

## 3. Disintegrants: how do they work?

Common mechanisms include:
- **Swelling:** the material absorbs water and expands, helping disrupt the tablet.
- **Wicking / capillary action:** liquid enters the tablet through pores and weakens interparticle bonds.
- **Deformation recovery:** particles recover their shape after compression and help break the compact.
- **Effervescence:** gas generation can assist breakup in appropriate formulations.

Superdisintegrants include sodium starch glycolate, croscarmellose sodium and crospovidone. Their performance depends on grade, concentration, formulation and manufacturing conditions.

## 4. Why too much lubricant can be a problem

Hydrophobic lubricants such as magnesium stearate can coat particles. Excessive concentration or prolonged blending may reduce interparticle bonding and slow wetting or dissolution.

<Callout type="exam" title="Common GPAT trap">
Magnesium stearate is primarily a **lubricant**, not a binder or disintegrant. Colloidal silicon dioxide is commonly used as a **glidant**.
</Callout>

## 5. Quick selection logic

- Need more tablet bulk? Consider a suitable diluent.
- Need granules to hold together? Select a binder.
- Need faster tablet breakup? Evaluate a disintegrant.
- Need easier ejection? Select a lubricant.
- Need better flow? Evaluate a glidant.

The final selection must consider compatibility, route, dose, stability, manufacturability and applicable quality requirements.

## Practice Questions

**Q1. Which excipient is commonly used as a glidant?**

A. Povidone  
B. Colloidal silicon dioxide  
C. Sodium starch glycolate  
D. Lactose

**Answer: B.** Colloidal silicon dioxide can improve powder flow.

**Q2. Which excipient class helps a tablet break apart after administration?**

A. Binder  
B. Lubricant  
C. Disintegrant  
D. Colouring agent

**Answer: C.** Disintegrants promote tablet breakup.

**Q3. Excessive magnesium stearate blending may cause:**

A. Improved wetting in every formulation  
B. Reduced tablet strength or slower dissolution in some formulations  
C. Increased API potency  
D. Conversion of a tablet into a capsule

**Answer: B.** Hydrophobic coating and over-lubrication can adversely affect bonding and release.

## Revision snapshot

- Diluent = bulk.
- Binder = cohesion.
- Disintegrant = breakup.
- Lubricant = reduced friction / easier ejection.
- Glidant = improved flow.
- Antiadherent = reduced sticking.
