## Start with the idea

Alkaloids are a large family of natural compounds that contain nitrogen. Many come from plants and some have strong effects on the body, which makes them important in pharmacognosy.

<Definition term="Alkaloids">Alkaloids are a diverse group of naturally occurring, basic nitrogen-containing organic compounds, commonly of plant origin, that often show marked physiological activity.</Definition>

The term covers a chemically diverse family. Many alkaloids contain nitrogen in a heterocyclic ring, but not every nitrogen-containing natural product is classified as an alkaloid.

## 1. Common properties

- Many are basic because of a nitrogen atom with an available lone pair.
- They often form salts with acids; salt formation can improve aqueous solubility.
- Many are bitter and physiologically active.
- Their solubility depends on pH, structure and salt form.
- Some are liquids, while many are crystalline solids.

## 2. Classification

A useful introductory classification is based on biosynthetic origin and the position / nature of nitrogen.

| Group | General description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| True alkaloids | Usually amino-acid-derived; nitrogen commonly in a heterocycle | Morphine, atropine, quinine |
| Protoalkaloids | Amino-acid-derived; nitrogen is not part of a heterocyclic ring | Ephedrine, mescaline |
| Pseudoalkaloids | Not directly derived from amino acids in the usual classification | Caffeine, solanidine |

Other classifications use the chemical nucleus: tropane, indole, quinoline, isoquinoline, pyridine/piperidine, purine and steroidal alkaloids.

## 3. Important examples

| Alkaloid | Source commonly taught | Chemical group / note |
|---|---|---|
| Atropine | Atropa belladonna | Tropane |
| Hyoscine (scopolamine) | Hyoscyamus niger | Tropane |
| Morphine | Papaver somniferum | Phenanthrene / isoquinoline-related alkaloid |
| Quinine | Cinchona species | Quinoline |
| Reserpine | Rauvolfia serpentina | Indole |
| Caffeine | Coffea / Theobroma species | Purine alkaloid |
| Ephedrine | Ephedra species | Protoalkaloid |

## 4. General identification tests

| Test | Reagent | Typical positive observation |
|---|---|---|
| Dragendorff | Potassium bismuth iodide reagent | Orange to reddish precipitate |
| Mayer | Potassium mercuric iodide reagent | Cream or pale precipitate |
| Wagner | Iodine in potassium iodide | Reddish-brown precipitate |
| Hager | Saturated picric acid solution | Yellow precipitate |

<Callout type="caution" title="A screening test is not proof">
These are general precipitation tests. They are not specific to one alkaloid and can give interference. Confirm identity using suitable chromatographic, spectroscopic and reference-standard methods.
</Callout>

## 5. Extraction principle

A simplified acid–base extraction concept:
1. Convert alkaloids into water-soluble salts using a suitable dilute acid.
2. Separate the aqueous phase from insoluble plant material.
3. Basify under controlled conditions to regenerate free-base alkaloids where chemically appropriate.
4. Extract the free base into a suitable immiscible organic solvent.
5. Purify and identify using validated methods.

The exact solvent, pH and purification strategy depend on the alkaloid and safety requirements.

## GPAT quick revision

- Dragendorff: orange / reddish precipitate.
- Mayer: cream precipitate.
- Wagner: reddish-brown precipitate.
- Hager: yellow precipitate.
- True alkaloids commonly have heterocyclic nitrogen.
- Caffeine is a purine alkaloid; atropine is a tropane alkaloid; quinine is a quinoline alkaloid.
