# Autonomic Nervous System: Receptor Map

The autonomic nervous system (ANS) regulates many involuntary functions, including heart rate, smooth-muscle activity and glandular secretion.

## Two major divisions

- **Sympathetic division:** commonly supports the body's response to stress or activity.
- **Parasympathetic division:** commonly supports routine maintenance functions such as digestion and glandular activity.

These are useful broad patterns; individual organs can have more complex regulation.

## Cholinergic receptors

Acetylcholine acts at muscarinic and nicotinic receptors.

| Receptor | Broad location/function | Main signalling |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | CNS, autonomic ganglia, gastric pathways | Gq |
| M2 | Heart | Gi |
| M3 | Glands and smooth muscle; vascular endothelium | Gq |
| Nm | Neuromuscular junction | Ligand-gated cation channel |
| Nn | Autonomic ganglia and adrenal medulla | Ligand-gated cation channel |

### Quick memory aid

- M1, M3 and M5: generally Gq-coupled.
- M2 and M4: generally Gi-coupled.
- Nicotinic receptors: ligand-gated ion channels.

## Adrenergic receptors

| Receptor | Major signalling | Common exam association |
|---|---|---|
| α1 | Gq | Smooth-muscle contraction, vasoconstriction |
| α2 | Gi | Reduced presynaptic neurotransmitter release |
| β1 | Gs | Increased heart rate and contractility |
| β2 | Gs | Bronchodilation and relaxation of selected smooth muscle |
| β3 | Gs | Lipolysis and detrusor relaxation |

The response depends on tissue, receptor density, dose and physiological state.

## Agonist versus antagonist

- **Agonist:** binds and activates a receptor.
- **Antagonist:** binds and blocks receptor activation by an agonist.
- **Partial agonist:** activates a receptor but produces a lower maximal response than a full agonist in the same system.

Do not assume all drugs within a class have identical selectivity or clinical effects.

## Selected drug examples

| Drug | Main receptor action (simplified) |
|---|---|
| Phenylephrine | α1 agonist |
| Clonidine | α2 agonist |
| Salbutamol | β2 agonist |
| Dobutamine | Predominantly β1 agonist |
| Atropine | Muscarinic antagonist |
| Propranolol | Non-selective β antagonist |

These are teaching examples; their clinical use, contraindications and dose depend on the approved product information and patient context.
