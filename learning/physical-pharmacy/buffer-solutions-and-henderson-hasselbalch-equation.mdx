## Start with the idea

A buffer is a solution that helps keep pH from changing too much when a small amount of acid or base is added. Buffers are useful when a medicine or test needs a controlled pH.

<Definition term="Buffer solution">A buffer is a solution that resists a large change in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added, or when the solution is diluted within suitable limits.</Definition>

A buffer commonly contains a weak acid and its conjugate base, or a weak base and its conjugate acid.

## 1. Types of buffers

| Buffer type | Components | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Acidic buffer | Weak acid + its salt with a strong base | Acetic acid + sodium acetate |
| Basic buffer | Weak base + its salt with a strong acid | Ammonia solution + ammonium chloride |

## 2. Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

For an acidic buffer:

<Formula label="Acidic buffer">pH = pKa + log ([salt] / [acid])</Formula>

For a basic buffer, one common form is:

<Formula label="Basic buffer">pOH = pKb + log ([salt] / [base])</Formula>

Then:

\[
pH + pOH = 14
\]

at 25 °C for dilute aqueous solutions (approximately).

## 3. Understand the ratio

If salt concentration equals acid concentration:

\[
\log(1)=0 \Rightarrow pH=pKa
\]

If the salt-to-acid ratio increases, pH rises. If the ratio decreases, pH falls.

**Example:** A weak acid has pKa = 4.76 and the salt-to-acid ratio is 10.

\[
pH = 4.76 + \log(10) = 5.76
\]

## 4. Buffer capacity

Buffer capacity describes the amount of strong acid or base required to change the pH of a defined quantity of buffer by one pH unit.

It generally increases with:
- Higher total buffer concentration.
- A conjugate acid/base ratio that is not too far from unity.

A buffer is most effective around its pKa, commonly within approximately pKa ± 1 as a practical rule of thumb.

## 5. Pharmaceutical significance

Buffers may be used to:
- Maintain formulation pH.
- Improve chemical stability when degradation is pH-dependent.
- Support drug solubility or ionisation.
- Reduce pH-related irritation, where appropriate.
- Maintain suitable conditions for analytical procedures.

<Callout type="caution" title="More buffer is not always better">
A high buffer capacity can make a formulation resist physiological pH adjustment. Buffer selection and concentration must balance stability, solubility, compatibility and tolerability.
</Callout>

## 6. Common exam traps

- pH = pKa when [salt] = [acid].
- A buffer does not keep pH absolutely constant; it resists change.
- Dilution can change buffer capacity even when the ratio and approximate pH remain similar.
- Buffer capacity and buffer pH are different concepts.
